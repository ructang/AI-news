**「Background」:** Homomorphic encryption \(HE\) is a cryptographic technique that allows computations to be performed on encrypted data without first decrypting it, preserving privacy while enabling data processing. Google has been researching ways to make HE practical for AI inference, and its latest effort, announced in the blog post, is HEIR, an open-source compiler added to its Private Computing Toolkit. Historically, HE has imposed a massive computational overhead—often more than 1,000 times slower than plaintext inference—which has limited its commercial viability, especially for large-scale AI workloads. However, advances like HEIR aim to reduce this overhead and open up possibilities for privacy-sensitive applications in regulated industries such as healthcare, finance, and government, where data confidentiality is critical.

**「Community Discussion」:** Hacker News commenters were skeptical about practical viability, with one researcher noting that homomorphic encryption and related techniques have overheads around 10^3 on inference tasks, making them not commercially viable. Several commenters also highlighted a perceived contradiction between Google&\#x27;s privacy messaging and its broader track record, such as its password manager lacking end-to-end encryption by default. Others pointed to running AI on personal hardware as a more private alternative, and one commenter shared the FHE textbook as a learning resource.
