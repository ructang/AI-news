### [LLM Benchmark Drift Analysis of 31k Hourly Scores](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1w1jp1j/i_analyzed_31352_hourly_llm_benchmark_scores/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An open-source continuous benchmarking system, AIStupidLevel, analyzed 31,352 hourly LLM benchmark scores across 49 model identifiers and multiple providers, finding within-day score variation of 2.8 points and between-day variation of 8.4 points. The roughly three-fold larger between-day swing suggests that comparing daily medians is a far more reliable signal for detecting production performance drift than reacting to hourly stochastic noise. The project runs coding, deep reasoning, tool-calling, and canary tasks repeatedly, with coding responses executed and tool-calling workflows validated in Docker sandboxes. The dataset has grown to 169,858 benchmark runs and 104,458 measured scores across 81 historical models, with 22 models currently monitored on six providers. The system has also flagged a 32% sustained performance decline in Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite as a critical incident, and it powers an OpenAI-compatible router that picks models based on current task-specific performance, stability, tool-calling reliability, latency, and cost. For AI application builders, this highlights that backend model quality is not static and that continuous, task-relevant evaluation is becoming an essential observability layer.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/ionutvi · Aug 29, 11:08

**「Background」:** Traditional LLM benchmarks rate models at a single point in time, which gives builders a snapshot of capability but no visibility into how a model behaves hour-to-hour or day-to-day in production. Models served behind APIs may undergo silent updates, experience infrastructure-side changes, or exhibit stochastic variation in generation quality, all of which can affect the outputs users actually see. Continuous evaluation addresses this by repeatedly running consistent tasks over time and applying statistical change-point detection to separate ordinary randomness from sustained degradation. The author of AIStupidLevel built this approach specifically to add a &quot;capability&quot; dimension to existing production monitoring tools, which typically only track availability, errors, latency, and token cost.
