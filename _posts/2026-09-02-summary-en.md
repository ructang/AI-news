### [TontaubeV1: open-weight TTS for expressive long-form narration](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1w4afjn/we_released_tontaubev1_a_characterlevel_tts_model/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

TontaubeV1, a 2.9B-parameter open-weight text-to-speech model focused on expressive long-form narration and low-latency local inference, has been released by TontaubeAI. It supports zero-shot voice cloning from up to one minute of reference audio and is primarily aimed at English and German, although it was trained on seven languages and roughly 200,000 hours of audio. Built on the DualCodec multi-codebook audio codec, the model uses character-level tokenization and a chunked position scheme to improve robustness with rare character sequences and to keep context bounded for very long passages. In a 400-passage LLM-as-a-judge audiobook benchmark, it scored 50.1% against ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 on prosody and was preferred over Fish Audio S2 Pro, Gradium, and Cartesia Sonic 3, though the developers note human listening tests remain the gold standard. The current release requires a GPU with at least 24 GB of VRAM, with quantized versions and fine-tuning support planned.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/EAVDR · Sep 1, 12:23

**「Background」:** Modern TTS models increasingly use LLM backbones with discrete audio codecs, where the model predicts audio tokens from text. Many such models rely on the backbone LLM&\#x27;s BPE tokenizer, which can tokenize rare character sequences into combinations that are underrepresented in TTS training data. TontaubeV1 instead forces Qwen-style tokenization into individual characters to simplify character-to-sound mapping, and it uses logical position IDs across chunked text and audio streams to keep long-form generation coherent. Open-weight releases like this matter for application builders because they enable local, low-latency inference and fine-tuning without relying on proprietary APIs, although the current VRAM requirement is still substantial.
